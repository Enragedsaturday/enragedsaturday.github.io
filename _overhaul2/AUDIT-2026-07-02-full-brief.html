1 · Executive verdict

The Overhaul-2 plan is fundamentally sound, and the corpus it will rebuild is in far better legal shape than the plan assumes. Every quantitative claim in the runbook that could be measured matched — several to the exact digit (43/47 slip-op files, 18 pipe-escape files, 457 case pages). Legal accuracy sampling found one substantive error in 14 verified pages, zero wrong holdings, and treatment honesty (Caniglia-limits-Cady, Lange-limits-Santana) handled, in the auditor's words, "unusually well." But the audit surfaced three critical defects that will corrupt downstream work if the next spec thread runs before they're fixed — one of which (the S2 denylist) would silently exclude Chatrie, a landmark geofence decision and core CSSI subject matter, from the Verified Authority Database. Two independent agents converged on its root cause from opposite directions.

3 · The three critical findings

CRITICAL 1  The S2 denylist is built on a misdiagnosis and would exclude Chatrie from the authority database

Where: S2-authority-database.spec.md R7 · CL-DATA-INVENTORY.md · content/cases/Chatrie v. United States.md:23,38,60,72

Overhaul 1 recorded CourtListener objects 10881683 (Chatrie) and 10813527 (Zorn) as "corrupted" — the fetched text came back as unrelated cases. The live-API audit refuted this: those are cluster IDs, and O1 fetched them against the opinions endpoint, where the same numbers happen to identify different, valid documents. Cluster 10881683 is the genuine Chatrie v. United States (SCOTUS, 2026-06-29, No. 25-112; the geofence-search holding was verified in its sub-opinion 11349205), and cluster 10813527 is the genuine Zorn v. Linton (SCOTUS, 2026-03-23). The S2 spec's "builder refuses these ids" rule would therefore block ingesting a landmark Fourth Amendment decision that is core CSSI subject matter — and the Chatrie case page itself carries a stale "DO NOT ingest 10881683" warning that would propagate the error into future pipeline runs. The case-law agent independently reproduced the same root cause from the content side.

Fix: drop or rework the denylist — namespace any entries by object type, document the real rule (never fetch /opinions/<cluster_id>), rely on R2's two-key identity check (which already catches this failure class), and backfill the Chatrie page with opinion_id: 11349205 and a corrected note.

CRITICAL 2  The runbook still orders S5/S7 to build the officer-BLUF box that the approved S1 spec bans

Where: RUNBOOK.md:44-46, 86-87, 199-201, 236-237 · PRACTICES.md:101-104 vs. S1-standards.spec.md R6 + Appendix C

The signed-off S1 spec bans the field-application / officer-bottom-line summary "for the whole project." Four separate runbook passages and PRACTICES §5 still direct the S5 and S7 threads to design exactly that artifact as a human-in-the-loop deliverable. A fresh spec thread — which by design reads the runbook first — would faithfully build a banned feature. This is compounded by a structural gap: O2 has four normative layers (runbook, practices, specs, wrappers) with live conflicts and no supremacy rule (Overhaul 1 had SR-3 for precisely this).

Fix: propagate S1's decision into all four runbook locations, add a supersession note to PRACTICES §5, and add a one-line precedence stack (specs > runbook > practices > wrappers, or as decided) to RUNBOOK §0.

CRITICAL 3  S6's scope and S2's manifest rest on an audit artifact that doesn't exist in the repo

Where: RUNBOOK.md:220 · S2-authority-database.spec.md:206-207

S6's entire scope is defined by "this file's earlier §S6 seed / audit_cases.py" — neither is anywhere in the repo. The approved S2 spec's Method step 1 seeds _manifest.json from this same nonexistent "S6 audit list." The ~70–80 named-but-no-page roster, the ~6 alias mismatches, and the ~5 citation placeholders from the 2026-07-01 audit exist only in a past session's memory. (The claims agent independently re-derived ~80–84 candidates, confirming the number is right — but the canonical list still needs committing.)

  1. The constitution isn't propagating. Every future spec thread reads the runbook first, so contradictions there are load-bearing. One fix class: propagate + precedence stack + supersession notes.

The two-key identity check already catches this class; trust it and document the root cause.

Meta-labels, rule-ID citations, "CL-confirm pending," slip-op pins, mid-line ^pin-N anchors (233 files), 51 table-header schemas: the machinery is visible on published pages. S1's rules cover pieces of this; the broadened R14 + LINT sizing should treat "reader never sees the factory" as one requirement.

  6. The corpus is better than the plan's mental model of it. Frontmatter is uniform, treatment honesty is real, currency is elite. That changes calibration: S2's projector is defensive (not cleanup), S7's lift is polish + structure (not rescue), and the audit's job downstream is protecting an already-high baseline.
