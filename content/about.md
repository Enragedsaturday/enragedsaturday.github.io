This is a **teaching reference on federal search-and-seizure law** — the case law of the
Fourth Amendment and its neighbors, organized for instructors and students, not for
litigation. It is **not legal advice**, and it is not a substitute for reading the
opinions themselves or for a commercial citator.

## How this reference is built

Every case page traces back to a **verified authority record**: the opinion located on
[CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/), its citation confirmed, quotes
string-matched against the opinion text, and pinpoints checked against the cited page.
Doctrine statements separate the **black-letter rule** from its explanation and its
supporting authorities, and the rule layer requires independent multi-reviewer approval
before it ships.

## How current is it?

Case law moves. Each case carries two dates, visible when you hover its treatment pill:

- **Content verified** — when the page's assertions were last checked against the opinion.
- **Treatment checked** — when we last looked for later cases that limit, criticize, or
  overrule it.

A green pill means we found the case still standing for the point we cite it for as of
that date — see [[2-legal-system-research/Verifying Good Law|Verifying Good Law]] for
exactly how that check runs. Overruled or superseded cases stay on the site as **history**,
clearly flagged, with a pointer to what replaced them.
