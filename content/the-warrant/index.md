# The Warrant

The default rule and its machinery: the Fourth Amendment prefers a warrant, and these pages cover what a valid one requires, how it is obtained, and how it must be carried out. A valid warrant rests on four things, all four required: **probable cause** shown to a **neutral and detached magistrate** on **oath or affirmation**, describing with **particularity** the place to be searched and the things to be seized. How the application is built, and how the warrant is carried out, each has its own body of rules.

- **[[Getting a Warrant]]** — building an application that survives later review: [[Probable Cause in the Affidavit|the affidavit's showing]], [[The Neutral and Detached Magistrate|the magistrate who must issue it]], [[Particularity|the precision the warrant must have on its face]], and [[Franks Challenges|the challenge to a warrant built on a false affidavit]].
- **[[Executing a Warrant]]** — carrying a valid warrant out lawfully: [[Knock-and-Announce|announcement at the door]], [[Detention and Search of Persons at the Scene|control of the people present]], and [[Scope Manner and Related Issues|the limits on how far and how forcefully the search may go]].
